# 1. Executive Summary

EthioBio AI currently provides a high-quality, curated educational knowledge base consisting of nationally aligned Biology textbooks that are ingested during development and indexed for retrieval.

This architecture has served as the authoritative knowledge source for tutoring and educational reasoning. However, future platform capabilities—including Guided Study, Lesson Planning, Classroom Management, School Administration, Research Assistance, Personalized Tutoring, and Multi-Agent Educational Reasoning—require the platform to consume multiple knowledge sources while preserving the curated textbook corpus as the authoritative foundation.

To support this evolution, the platform shall introduce a **Knowledge Management Layer (KML)**.

The Knowledge Management Layer becomes the central subsystem responsible for managing all educational knowledge assets regardless of origin.

Rather than treating uploaded files as isolated resources, the platform treats every educational artifact as a structured **Knowledge Object** managed throughout its lifecycle.

---

# 3. Architectural Principles

The following principles govern every future implementation.

## Principle 1

Core educational content is immutable.

Developer-ingested textbooks remain read-only.

No user action may alter the official corpus.

---

## Principle 2

All educational knowledge is represented uniformly.

Whether the source is

* textbook
* lesson plan
* worksheet
* exam
* curriculum
* image
* notebook
* research paper

the platform represents it internally as a **Knowledge Object**.

---

## Principle 3

Knowledge is layered.

Instead of one corpus, the platform manages multiple knowledge layers.

---

## Principle 4

Knowledge is discoverable.

Every Knowledge Object is indexed through semantic, lexical, and metadata-based retrieval.

---

## Principle 5

Knowledge is explainable.

Every response generated by agents must retain traceability to supporting evidence.

---

## Principle 6

Knowledge processing is asynchronous.

User uploads should never block retrieval or interaction.

---

## Principle 7

Retrieval is planner-driven.

The retrieval layer never decides independently.

The Planner Agent determines:

* which sources
* retrieval depth
* retrieval strategy
* confidence thresholds

# 5. Knowledge Management Layer (KML)

The KML becomes a new platform subsystem positioned between planners and storage systems.

Responsibilities include:

* Knowledge registration
* Document lifecycle
* Metadata management
* Workspace management
* Versioning
* Processing orchestration
* Retrieval orchestration
* Citation tracking
* Knowledge governance

Importantly, the KML does **not** perform reasoning itself. It prepares and governs knowledge so that planners and reasoning agents can consume it consistently.

---

The KML is composed of several services.

## Knowledge Registry

The system of record for every Knowledge Object.

Stores:

* identity

* relationships

## Workspace Service

Manages:

## Knowledge Processing Service

Responsible for:

## Metadata Service

Maintains:

* educational metadata
* semantic metadata
* administrative metadata

---

## Retrieval Gateway

Provides a unified retrieval interface regardless of storage backend.

It abstracts vector search, BM25, graph traversal, and metadata filtering behind a common contract.

---

## Citation Service

Maintains traceability between generated responses and supporting evidence.

This is especially important for educational trust and explainability.

# 7. Integration with Existing Architecture

The new KML integrates with, rather than replaces, existing systems.

* **Ingestion** modules become processors managed by the Knowledge Processing Service.
* **RAG** components become retrieval engines behind the Retrieval Gateway.
* **Memory** continues to manage conversational and learner context but becomes another knowledge layer.
* **Graph orchestration** remains the reasoning engine, now receiving structured evidence from the KML instead of querying individual stores directly.
* **Evaluation** and hallucination detection continue to operate downstream, validating synthesized responses against cited evidence.

This preserves the substantial investment already made in your current architecture while introducing a cleaner separation of concerns.

# 9. Design Constraints

To maintain long-term stability, the following constraints apply:

* The built-in textbook corpus remains immutable.
* User content never overwrites or modifies platform knowledge.
* Retrieval must be source-aware and preserve provenance.
* All Knowledge Objects are versioned.
* Long-running processing tasks are asynchronous and resumable.
* Services communicate through stable interfaces rather than direct storage access.

---

# 10. Scalability Strategy

The architecture is designed to scale along three dimensions:

* **Subjects**: Biology today; Chemistry, Physics, Mathematics, History, and others later.
* **Roles**: Students, teachers, schools, administrators, researchers.
* **Knowledge Types**: Documents today; images, audio, video, datasets, simulations, and interactive resources in future releases.

No core architectural changes should be required when new subjects or knowledge types are introduced.

---

# 11. Migration Strategy

The existing biology textbook corpus becomes the initial Platform Knowledge layer without requiring reorganization.

The migration path is:

1. Register the existing corpus in the Knowledge Registry.
2. Preserve existing embeddings and retrieval indices where possible.
3. Introduce layered retrieval without changing current tutoring behavior.
4. Enable workspace knowledge as an additive capability.
5. Gradually route all retrieval through the Retrieval Gateway.

This minimizes disruption while allowing incremental adoption.

---

# 12. Success Criteria

The architecture will be considered successful when it enables:

* A unified representation for all educational knowledge.
* Seamless coexistence of curated textbooks and user-managed content.
* Planner-driven retrieval across multiple knowledge layers.
* Explainable, evidence-backed responses with source attribution.
* Reuse of the same knowledge infrastructure across tutoring, lesson planning, classroom management, school administration, and future educational features.
* Expansion to new subjects and resource types without architectural redesign.

# 13. As-Built System Architecture Reference (As of July 2026 Implementation)

During execution, the target architecture was implemented with the following characteristics:

### 13.1 Strangler Fig Adoption
The KML layers and database services are built in parallel with the legacy RAG pipeline. New features route queries through the KML Retrieval Gateway, while legacy nodes (`RetrievalNode`, `SearchFanoutNode`, `TutorNode`) maintain their existing pathways through the `VectorStoreAdapter`. This guarantees 100% legacy application stability.

### 13.2 Logical Layers & Routing
Rather than physically separating layers, the 7 proposed layers are represented logically as a `layer` field on Knowledge Objects and indexed chunks. The Retrieval Gateway and Knowledge Router dynamically filter vector database query bounds by this field using workspace contextual constraints.

### 13.3 Monolithic Service Contract (KASCS logical packaging)
To prevent complex deployment overhead, KASCS services are implemented as logical Python packages inside the existing monolithic backend under `src/core/`. Each package exposes clean boundaries (e.g. `src/core/workspace/`, `src/core/knowledge_registry/`) using `__init__.py` facades.
